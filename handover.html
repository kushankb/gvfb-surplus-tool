For everyone
What the Tool Does

Every year, a significant share of BC's fresh produce never reaches anyone's plate — it is rejected at the farm, overstocked at stores, or discarded before purchase. This tool estimates how much of that surplus could be recovered, and puts it in human terms: how many people could eat for a year if that food were redirected.

It covers 40 fruits and vegetables consumed in British Columbia, from 2010 to 2025, including BC-grown crops, interprovincial shipments, and international imports circulating in BC's supply chain.

Four tabs in the dashboard
  • Overview Total recoverable surplus for a selected year, broken down by crop. Key figures: total weight, upstream vs downstream split, and how many people that could feed for a year.
  • Regions & Seasons A map of BC's farming regions showing where surplus originates, and a seasonal chart showing monthly patterns.
  • By Produce Year-over-year trends for any individual crop — useful for tracking how a commodity's surplus has changed over time.
  • Farm Gate Procured What GVFB has actually collected from BC farms (Feb 2024 – Mar 2026) compared to the estimated available surplus. Shows the "capture rate" — how much of the potential is being reached — for each crop.

The About this tool button in the top right of the dashboard contains a full plain-English methodology explanation, including data sources and limitations.

How the surplus estimates are calculated
  • Upstream surplus (farm-gate): applied as a two-stage cascade to BC StatsCan harvested quantities — 14.2% production loss is taken first, then 8.7% postharvest loss is applied to what remains (not summed: the effective combined rate is ~21.7%, not 23%). "Harvested quantity" already excludes field losses from weather and pests — this model captures what was successfully harvested but rejected for cosmetic or marketability reasons.
  • Downstream surplus (retail): a 2.5% distribution + 5.8% retail loss is applied to BC's provincial retail supply. Retail supply is derived from a full mass balance:

    BC production − upstream surplus + international imports + interprovincial inflows − exports − interprovincial outflows

    Interprovincial flows are not directly observed — they are estimated using Inverse Distance Weighting (IDW), allocating surplus from supply provinces to deficit provinces by road proximity. This methodology is consistent with Canada's Food Flow tool.
  • People fed: total surplus ÷ 204 kg — Canada's Food Guide benchmark of 7 servings × 80 g × 365 days per person per year.
Important caveat on loss rates: The same loss rates are applied to all 40 produce types. We acknowledge that highly perishable produce (e.g. berries) typically has higher loss rates than hardy produce (e.g. potatoes). We found too few category-specific observations to justify separate rates, and Second Harvest (2024) — while implied to have subcategory-level rates from extensive interviews — has declined to share those figures. The estimates should be read as indicative, not precise.

Loss rates were reviewed and developed by Brian Lee (GVFB), drawing on seven studies and three self-reports from existing GVFB farm donors.
Downscaling BC consumption from national data

FAOSTAT provides food supply accounts at the national (Canada) level only. To estimate BC-specific consumption, national per-capita figures are multiplied by BC's population (Statistics Canada annual estimates). This assumes BC residents consume at the national average rate — a simplification that is reasonable at the provincial scale but does not capture regional dietary differences.

How this could be improved:

  • BC-specific dietary survey data (e.g. from CCHS Nutrition) could replace national per-capita averages for produce where regional patterns differ significantly.
  • Retail scanner data or grocery chain sales data for BC would give a more direct consumption signal, removing the need for per-capita downscaling entirely.
Deriving seasonal patterns from annual estimates

StatsCan production data and the surplus model both produce annual totals. To show monthly and seasonal patterns in the dashboard, annual figures are disaggregated using two seasonal signal sources:

  • GVFB Farm-to-Community donation data — the months in which GVFB actually receives each crop from farms, used as a proxy for the BC harvest window.
  • BC Ministry of Agriculture harvest calendars — published typical harvest months by commodity, used to fill gaps where GVFB data is thin.

The annual estimate for each crop is distributed across its active harvest months in proportion to the seasonal weights, giving a monthly approximation. This is an allocation, not a measurement — actual month-to-month variation within the season is not captured.

How this could be improved:

  • StatsCan publishes some monthly greenhouse production data — incorporating this would give a directly observed seasonal signal for tomatoes, peppers, and cucumbers.
  • As the GVFB FTC dataset grows in future years, its seasonal patterns will become a more reliable signal of actual farm availability windows.
  • Farmer-reported harvest start/end dates, collected through GVFB's procurement relationships, could provide crop- and region-specific windows rather than provincial averages.
Downscaling provincial production to Census Agricultural Regions (CARs)

The Regions & Seasons tab shows surplus broken down by BC's Census Agricultural Regions — sub-provincial areas defined by Statistics Canada. StatsCan production data is reported at the provincial level, so it must be disaggregated spatially to produce the CAR-level estimates shown on the map.

This is done using Census of Agriculture area-harvested shares: for each produce type, the fraction of BC's total harvested area located within each CAR (from the 2021 Census of Agriculture) is used to allocate the provincial surplus estimate. For example, if the Fraser Valley CAR contains 60% of BC's strawberry area, it is assigned 60% of BC's estimated strawberry surplus.

How this could be improved:

  • Yield heterogeneity — the current method assumes uniform yields across CARs. Using CAR-level yield data where available (e.g. from the Census of Agriculture or BC Ministry of Agriculture surveys) would produce more accurate spatial estimates.
  • More recent census data — the 2026 Census of Agriculture will update harvested area shares; incorporating it when released would improve spatial accuracy.
  • Crop-specific regional patterns — some crops are highly concentrated in specific CARs (e.g. Okanagan tree fruit, Delta greenhouse); targeted data collection for those crops could replace the area-share proxy.
Key interpretation notes
  • 2024–2025 data is preliminary — the tool flags this visually. Figures will update as StatsCan revises.
  • The capture rate on the Farm Gate tab (GVFB collected ÷ estimated surplus) reflects how much of the potential is being reached per crop. A low rate is expected for widely dispersed crops like blueberries — it reflects logistical access, not underperformance.
  • Downstream surplus includes all food wasted within BC's retail system, including produce grown elsewhere. This is the full system picture, not just BC-grown food.
For technical staff
Architecture & Maintenance

The dashboard is a static single-page React application. There is no backend, no database, and no API. All data is bundled as JSON files. Updating the tool means replacing those JSON files and redeploying — no server configuration required.

⚠ Data sensitivity — gvfbProcured.json

This file contains GVFB's internal farm procurement records (Feb 2024 – Mar 2026) including farm names, products, dates, and weights. It is currently bundled in the public GitHub repository. GVFB should review whether this level of detail is appropriate for public disclosure, and consider removing or anonymising this file from the public repo if not.

Updating the data
  1. Regenerate the JSON files from the upstream Python data pipeline.
  2. Replace the files in src/data/.
  3. Push to main — GitHub Actions will rebuild and redeploy automatically once a live deployment is configured.

Deploy workflow: .github/workflows/deploy.yml · Note: the tool is not currently deployed to a live URL.

Known technical issue — Recharts animation

The Farm Gate capture rate chart uses isAnimationActive={false} on the <Bar> component. This is intentional and must not be removed. Without it, Recharts 3 renders all bars as invisible when <Cell> children are present for per-bar colour. This is a library bug, not a data issue.

Ongoing Support & Future Work

This handover marks the completion of the initial version of the tool. Ongoing support is available and further development is anticipated, including possibilities for scaling this work.

Immediate next steps
  • Deploy the tool to a publicly accessible URL (to be arranged with GVFB).
  • Review data sensitivity of gvfbProcured.json in the public GitHub repository.
  • Update procurement data as new farm gate collection records become available beyond March 2026.
  • Refresh annual data once StatsCan releases final 2024–2025 production figures.
Potential future extensions
  • Crop-specific loss rates — if subcategory-level data becomes available, the model can be updated to reflect perishability differences across produce types.
  • Expanded procurement coverage — integrate additional years of GVFB farm data as they accumulate.
  • Donor-facing outputs — adapted visualisations for grant applications, annual reports, or fundraising materials.
  • Regional disaggregation — connect surplus estimates more directly to specific farming regions or GVFB procurement geographies.
  • Other provincial adaptations — the methodology is transferable to other provinces with equivalent StatsCan and trade data.
